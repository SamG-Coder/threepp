# Neon Downtown Rain — modular Grok image contract

Every raster file in `assets/` must be generated by Grok. The runtime may key,
crop, arrange, tint, animate, light and reflect those images, but it must not
draw replacement scenery or manufacture raster art. This library is a reusable
front-elevation construction kit, not a collection of finished street scenes.

## The place and shared image language

Build an original, dense 1980s metropolitan downtown after midnight: aging
concrete, brick and tile, narrow businesses, service infrastructure, buses,
taxis, umbrellas and evidence of ordinary life. The look is cinematic painted
realism with photographic material detail and restrained brush texture. Use one
consistent cool blue-black night ambience and an upper-camera-left key. Runtime
lighting, weather and reflections create the rain-soaked final scene.

This is not a port district: no harbor, docks, boats, seawalls, cargo terminals,
maritime warehouses or nautical imagery. Do not reproduce real brands, famous
landmarks, copyrighted characters or franchise marks.

## Master rule for every non-surface source

- Use a straight-on, face-on orthographic front elevation. Vertical and
  horizontal axes stay parallel. No three-quarter view, vanishing point,
  perspective foreshortening, visible side wall, visible roof plane or ground.
- Place exactly one complete, isolated subject in each file, centered on both
  axes with its intended pivot on the vertical centerline. Do not clip it.
- Fill every absent area and every legitimate opening with exact hot magenta
  `#ff00ff`. Leave at least 6% uninterrupted magenta on every outside edge.
- Include no floor, pavement, horizon, studio sweep, turf disc, plinth, paper,
  frame, caption, palette, contact patch, baked ground or baked shadow.
- Include no baked puddle, wet sheen, rain streak, neon reflection or wet
  highlight. Runtime materials, lights, weather cards and reflectors add them.
- Keep scale, baseline, elevation, material response and light direction
  consistent within each group so modules can be exchanged without redrawing.
- Reserve the lower-right 24% by 12% as uninterrupted `#ff00ff`, with no subject
  pixels. Grok's unavoidable source overlay may land only in this sacrificial
  field; the runtime removes that field before the card is used. Include no
  other watermark, UI, signature, readable prose or real branding. Original
  signs should use one clear pictogram or short mark.
- Keep `#ff00ff` out of opaque subject pixels. Use violet-red or cyan instead of
  hot magenta in signs, clothing and practical lights so keying is unambiguous.
- Never generate a whole street, panorama, skyline panel, finished building,
  multi-business façade, prop cluster, object pile or people group.

For this contract, vehicle and walker “front elevation” means a perfectly
square-on orthographic broadside/profile: still a flat card with no view onto
the roof, front depth or ground. Weather files are flat orthographic effect
plates rather than photographed scenes.

## Neutral surface sources

The ten `surfaces` files are the only projection exception. They are square,
top-down, genuinely seamless neutral base textures with even diffuse lighting.
They must look dry before runtime shading: no puddles, rain, wet highlights,
specular sheen, storefront color, neon reflection, vehicle, cast shadow or
unique litter. Asphalt, tile, concrete, roof gravel, curb, lane paint and drain
grate sources describe only neutral material/albedo structure.

`wetness-breakup-mask.png` is not painted wet scenery. It is a seamless
grayscale material mask only: black-to-white breakup values, no color, object,
reflection, highlight, perspective or recognizable puddle silhouette. Runtime
uses it to vary wetness.

Keep important repeat structure away from the outer 14% source border. Grok's
source overlay may occupy that discarded border; the runtime trims it and uses
mirror-repeat without revealing a seam.

## Modular architecture groups

### Background structures

Each file is one reusable tower slab, rooftop utility room, rail span, rail
support, alley wall, gate or stair tower. Show the complete isolated unit. A
rail span may include only its structurally attached beamwork; a pier is a
separate file. Never combine several towers into a skyline or several alley
elements into a finished rear street.

### Blank building shells

Each building shell is one front-facing structural wall body. It may carry
subtle neutral construction seams, grime and material wear, but no installed
storefront, window unit, door insert, sign, awning, prop, person, pavement,
rain, glow or reflection. Leave usable blank frontage so the runtime can compose
different addresses from the same shell.

### Storefront inserts

Each storefront is one complete ground-floor business insert sized to sit over
a shell: frame, entrance and display glazing only. It may show restrained stock
silhouettes appropriate to its named business, but has no upper floors, street,
sign, awning, loose prop, person, glow spill or baked reflection. The twelve
businesses must differ structurally rather than being palette swaps.

### Window units

Each file contains exactly one window with only its integral sash, trim and
optional curtains or wired glass. No surrounding façade section, window row,
sign, planter, air conditioner, interior person or exterior fire escape. Keep
the six units dimensionally compatible with the blank shells.

### Signs

Each file contains exactly one sign and only its minimal attached housing or
bracket. No sign clusters, storefront, wall, cable bundle or second placard.
Use a single original pictogram or very short fictional mark; avoid generated
paragraphs. The sign must remain readable after chroma keying, with no
`#ff00ff` inside opaque tubing or glow cores.

### Awnings and canopies

Each file contains one complete canopy or awning and only its integral support
hardware. No storefront, sign, hanging stock, people, street or second awning.

## Props

Each prop file contains one movable object or one inseparable manufactured
fixture. A double-headed streetlamp remains one fixture; a vending pair, pile
of trash and crates, planter plus bench, or row of barriers is forbidden.
Separate machines, bins, crates, planters, benches and barriers into individual
files. A fire escape may retain its attached ladder. The utility pole and the
overhead cable span are separate assets; neither may include the other or a
second independently placeable object.

## Vehicles

Each vehicle file contains one complete vehicle in square-on orthographic
broadside with both wheels on one shared baseline. No driver card, road,
contact shadow, spray, reflected street, neighboring vehicle or visible roof
plane. Match wheel diameter, body height and scale across opposing directions.
Taxi, bus and delivery-van left/right versions are separately authored: doors,
lamps and abstract markings must remain physically correct, never mechanically
mirrored text or asymmetric hardware.

## Walker atlases

Each of the six walker files is a 4-column by 2-row atlas: eight evenly sized,
fixed-registration frames of the same full-body subject walking toward
screen-right. Body scale, center, elevation and foot baseline are identical in
all eight cells. Do not add a second person; every atlas is exactly one walker,
including the office worker carrying an umbrella.

The grid begins at the full raster's upper-left origin and occupies exactly the
upper-left 75% of raster width by 87% of raster height. Divide that safe region
into four equal columns and two equal rows. Keep hot-magenta separation inside
every cell. The remaining right 25% and bottom 13% of the full raster must be
uninterrupted `#ff00ff`, with no subject, label or guide. Grok's source overlay
may occupy this sacrificial region because the runtime crops the complete
region away before frame extraction.
Frame extraction must use those declared safe-grid bounds, never the whole
canvas. Leftward travel may mirror extracted runtime frames.

`walk-streetwise-player.png` is the original adult lead: a self-possessed night
wanderer in a charcoal raincoat or worn leather jacket, dark trousers and
grounded shoes. It must not copy an existing game or film character and carries
no visible weapon.

## Static people

Each static-person file contains one full-body adult only, in face-on or exact
orthographic profile as appropriate to the pose. No crowd, couple, queue,
conversation partner, furniture, shelter, doorway, counter or baked ground.
Every person has a clear foot baseline and the same scale family as the walkers.

## Weather and grounding plates

Each weather file contains one reusable flat effect only: rain streak pattern,
window-rain pattern, mist bank, tire-spray effect, ripple atlas or soft contact
mask. Do not include scenery, vehicles, feet, street, window frame or reflected
signs. Color effect plates use the same magenta absent field and safe margin;
material masks use a uniform zero-value field. The ripple atlas uses the same
upper-left 75% by 87% 4-by-2 safe-grid rule as walker atlases.

## Immediate rejection

- Any perspective-room box, isolated 3D render on a floor, miniature, toy,
  diorama, voxel object, clay model or visible extrusion.
- More than one independently placeable subject in a file.
- Any skyline panorama, whole street, finished building or finished façade.
- Installed windows, storefronts, signs or awnings baked into a building shell.
- Any baked wetness, puddle, rain, reflection, light spill or ground shadow.
- Subject pixels inside the outside safe margin or atlas-reserved margins.
- A source overlay or watermark surviving the declared runtime trim/crop, or
  any border, caption or UI mark outside the sacrificial source field.
- Inconsistent scale, baseline, elevation or front/profile orientation within a
  reusable group or opposing vehicle pair.
- Garbled prominent text, real brand, franchise mark or copied character.
